Slacking with Swift

His back is turned.
Your score is climbing.

Paul has his back turned, and every second you keep your finger on the screen is worth more than the last. He can turn around whenever he likes. Let go a moment too early and you keep almost nothing. Hold to the last possible instant and you walk away with ten times more.

It’s red light, green light, except the light is Paul, and he can see your screen.

A party game for people who know Paul

What you get

  • Letting go in time always works

    There are exactly two ways Paul catches you. You were still holding when he finished turning, or you reached for your phone while he was already looking. That’s the entire list. Nothing samples your finger, nothing guesses.

  • You always know how long you’ve got

    The instant he starts to move, the screen says LET GO and a bar drains in front of you. Early on that’s nearly a full second. Once he’s warmed up it’s about a third of one. Cutting it fine is worth real points, so the bar isn’t a warning — it’s an invitation.

  • Playing it safe costs you

    Your multiplier climbs for every second you hold and resets the moment you let go. Play cautiously and you’ll scrape a couple of thousand. Hold your nerve and you’ll clear ten times that. Anyone can survive Slacking with Swift. Almost nobody can score.

  • He gets faster, and he lies

    Over your first twenty turns Paul speeds up and his patience runs out. He also starts faking — twitching like he’s about to turn, purely to catch anyone who flinches. Nothing runs to a pattern. Every wait is a different length, so there’s nothing to memorise and everything to read.

  • No ads. No purchases. No account.

    Nothing interrupts a run, because there’s nothing in this app capable of interrupting one. No ad in the middle of a hold. No sign-in, no network, nothing to buy. Open it, play it, put it down.

  • One finger, one spare minute

    The whole screen is the button. Press and hold to earn, let go to survive. No tapping, no shaking, nothing to learn. A run lasts seconds, so it fits in a queue, a lift, or the last minute before a meeting starts. Your best score is saved. Beating it is your problem.

There are no levels in Slacking with Swift. There’s just Paul, a reflex game built around one held finger, and the growing certainty that he knows exactly what you should be doing instead.

Starring Paul Hudson, with his blessing.
